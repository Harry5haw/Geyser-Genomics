From a sequencer's raw reads to the places where one genome differs

Each of us carries a few million small differences from the human reference genome: the variants. Nearly all are harmless, but a handful can explain a rare disease, guide a cancer treatment or show how populations are related. Finding them reliably among billions of letters is the job of a variant-calling pipeline. Geyser is one: upload the reads, and it returns the variants, checked for quality, on a page like the ones on this site.

Why the variants matter

  • Rare disease. Many rare conditions come down to a single change in one gene. Sequencing a patient's genome and calling the variants is how the candidates are found.
  • Cancer. Tumours acquire their own variants, and knowing which are present can point to the treatments most likely to work.
  • Population genomics. Comparing variants across many people shows how traits and diseases are inherited, and how reference genomes can better represent everyone.

Geyser is an independent engineering and research project. Its results are for research use only, not for diagnosis.

What happens to an upload

  1. Upload. A gzipped FASTQ file (or the two files of a paired-end run) lands in storage, and that alone starts the run.
  2. Quality control and alignment, side by side. fastp reports on the reads' quality while bwa-mem2 places every read on the reference genome (minimap2 for PacBio HiFi and Oxford Nanopore long reads).
  3. Variant calling. bcftools by default, split into regions that run in parallel; or DeepVariant, Google's deep-learning caller, on a cloud GPU. DeepVariant looks at the evidence at each position as an image and classifies it with a neural network.

  4. The quality gate. A run can finish and still not be good enough. A small Rust program checks read quality, mapping, duplicates, depth and more against calibrated thresholds, and names every check that failed.
  5. Optional AI annotation. AlphaGenome, Google DeepMind's model, predicts how selected variants may change gene regulation. This step is optional and for research use only.

  6. A page per run. The calls, the QC report, the gate's verdict and a full run record (inputs, reference, tool versions, every stage's timing) are published here when the run ends.

What makes it different

  • Measured against the truth. Accuracy is scored with hap.py against the Genome in a Bottle (GIAB) truth sets, the community standard. On a whole human genome (HG003, 30x), DeepVariant made 57% fewer SNP errors and 90% fewer indel errors than bcftools (F1 0.9959 and 0.9950).
  • Fast and cheap in the cloud. A whole genome takes about 3 hours from reads to variants (2 h 59 min). One chromosome takes about 22 minutes and about 7 cents at AWS on-demand list prices. Nothing runs, or costs, between samples.
  • More than one kind of sequencing. Short reads, and long reads from PacBio HiFi and Oxford Nanopore. On PacBio HiFi (HG002, chromosome 20) the long-read route scored F1 0.9995 for SNPs and 0.9925 for indels.
  • Pangenome-aware, benchmarked. A pangenome reference combines many people's genomes instead of one. Geyser benchmarks pangenome-aware DeepVariant on GIAB samples on a local GPU, and offers graph alignment with vg giraffe locally and in the cloud.
  • Offline first. Every stage runs in the same container on a laptop against a folder, and in AWS against S3. That is how it is developed and tested: the cloud is a checkpoint, not the development loop.

Every number here comes from the project's own runs, recorded in its results and release notes.

Reading the 3D figures

The Results in 3D page draws six measurements from the project's own runs as landscapes you can turn over.

  • Each ridge is one thing being compared: a sample's alignment, a read file, or a setting, named in the key beside it. Across the figure runs the position on the chromosome, the sequencing cycle, or the setting tried.
  • Height and colour both show the value: blue is low and red is high (in the high-contrast theme, dark to light). Drag to rotate, scroll or pinch to zoom, double-click to reset; the 2D button gives a flat view, and every value is in the figure's CSV.
  • Coverage: how many reads cover each stretch of chromosome 20. Good is a flat plain at the sequencing depth (about 30×); spikes are repeats where reads pile up.
  • Base quality: how sure the sequencer was of each letter, cycle by cycle. Good is high and level, falling gently towards the end of each read. Phred 30 means one error in 1,000 bases.
  • DeepVariant tuning, cost and turnaround: how long the GPU caller takes for each setting, and what one cloud run costs and how long it takes for each machine size. Lower is better.

For specialists: GRCh38 chromosome 20, GIAB HG003 and HG005. Coverage is mean depth in 500 kb windows per alignment (short-read and vg giraffe); base quality is fastp's per-cycle mean before filtering; DeepVariant times are HG003 35x on a local RTX 3080; cost and turnaround are medians of 25 cloud runs of HG003 30x at Fargate on-demand list prices.
